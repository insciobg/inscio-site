You've outgrown QuickBooks and spreadsheets. You want one system to run the business. NetSuite keeps coming up. Then the doubts start.

"We're too small for NetSuite." "It costs too much to implement and run." "We tried it before, and it wasn't a good fit."

We hear all three from growing businesses every week. They're fair concerns. They're usually not about NetSuite. They're about how NetSuite gets sold and delivered.

## Why NetSuite feels too big

NetSuite is not only for big companies. Many of our clients are growing businesses, including manufacturers, distributors, and service companies, and a lot of them run lean. Plenty of them don't have a dedicated IT team. You can read some of their [stories here](/case-studies/).

Usually, what makes NetSuite feel big is how it's delivered. It tends to go one of two ways.

**The bare-minimum route.** It gets sold, and a remote team sets it up with minimal services. No one takes the time to understand your business. No one makes sure your team knows how to run it.

**The big-transformation route.** It gets sold, and a large systems integrator redesigns every process in a "digital transformation" that never seems to end.

Neither one is built for a small business.

## What it should look like

Here's the honest answer. If you're a small business, you should be up and running in a few months with a few hundred hours of services. Your team should be well trained and self-sufficient. And you should be seeing a real return on the investment.

From there, you start using what you bought NetSuite for: one system to run the business, workflows that save time, and AI in your everyday work. Then you keep improving, one step at a time.

Go-live is not the finish line. It is the foundation.

## What actually drives the cost

Cost comes down to three things: licensing, implementation, and what it takes to run the system after go-live. Each one depends on choices you make up front.

**Licensing.** What you pay each year depends on the right edition, the right modules, and the right number of users. It's easy to overbuy. We help clients size their licensing to what they actually need and negotiate the commercial terms. For realistic ranges and what to watch for, see our [NetSuite pricing guide](/blog/netsuite-pricing-guide). And if you're wondering whether buying direct gets you a better deal, it doesn't. [Here's why](/blog/netsuite-direct-vs-solution-provider).

**Implementation.** We size the scope to what you need today and offer the rest as options for later. That's on purpose. You can't get to where you want to go without a solid foundation. We think of it in three steps:

1. **Replace.** Do everything you do today, in NetSuite.
2. **Enhance.** Get the lift NetSuite gives you out of the box: better visibility, cleaner processes, less manual work.
3. **Extend.** Build on that foundation when you're ready. That doesn't have to mean more licenses. It might be a one-day project to do something you always needed but never could.

We've also built AI into how we deliver, for things like data preparation and project deliverables. That cuts the hours you pay for. Better services in less time.

**After go-live.** You don't need to hire a system admin. Many of our clients run NetSuite with little or no ongoing support. Others want to keep improving, so they use our Navigator Services for projects and new initiatives. It costs a fraction of a new hire, with a predictable monthly cost. Either way, it's your call.

## What if it didn't work last time?

Some of the people we talk to have lived through a NetSuite project that went badly. We take that seriously. In our experience, the software is rarely the whole story. Scope kept growing. The partner didn't understand the business. Customizations rebuilt old habits instead of using what NetSuite does well. The team went live without knowing how to run it.

A bad project is not proof NetSuite is the wrong fit. It is information. If that's your story, we want to hear it. What didn't work tells us exactly what to do differently.

## The bottom line

The question isn't whether you're big enough for NetSuite. It's whether your project is sized for your business, and whether your partner will tell you the truth about what you need.
